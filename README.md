## Works

### Sun: 72 Poems for 72 Solar Eclipses

[triptych](https://github.com/bergholt/triptych)

Ten words, two word orders, seventy-two poems: one for each solar eclipse of Saros 126, a cycle running from 1179 to 2459. The eclipse of 12 August 2026 is the forty-eighth. A program in Prolog makes the poem's constraints explicit and generates every combination they permit.

**In press:** *Sun: 72 Poems for 72 Solar Eclipses* (Forlaget Akse, 2026/2027). Each of the 72 variants on its own page with its eclipse, its letters and its partials over an A2 drone, followed by the complete source code. Paperback, 84 pages.

[About the work](https://bergholt.net/sun)

### Flora Excursoria Hafniensis

**[flora-excursoria-hafniensis](https://github.com/bergholt/flora-excursoria-hafniensis)** 

A transcription of the 903-name index to Drejer’s 1838 flora of Copenhagen, read with Prolog. The index interfiles Latin and Danish names in one alphabet and offers no translations. A plant’s two names were set on the same page, however, so the page number works as a join key and part of the pairing can be recovered.

[About the work](https://bergholt.net/flora)

### EIN. MEER. VON. SAND.

**[sand](https://github.com/bergholt/sand)**

A mid-90's style MS-DOS demo in Turbo Pascal and APL. The Danish and German inscriptions of the Sand Drift Monument at Tisvilde, erected in 1738, are read alongside English translations. Each letter selects a harmonic of A2 through a modulus that moves from twelve to seventeen. Characters leave the text and settle into a sea below it, while their earlier tunings remain in the drone.

The repository brings together the Pascal and APL source, DOS executable, soundtrack, ANSI artwork and DOSBox configuration, alongside notes on the work’s structure and preservation.
